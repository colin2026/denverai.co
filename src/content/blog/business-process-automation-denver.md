Most Denver small businesses are running on manual processes that should have been automated years ago. Chasing down leads by hand. Copying data between systems. Sending the same follow-up email over and over. None of that requires a human - it requires a system.

Business process automation is the practice of replacing repetitive, rules-based work with software that does it automatically, consistently, and without forgetting.

## What counts as a business process you can automate

Any task that follows a consistent pattern is a candidate. In Denver small businesses, that usually looks like:

- **Lead follow-up**: A new inquiry comes in, and someone has to manually send a response, add them to a spreadsheet, and remember to follow up in three days. Automatable.
- **Appointment reminders**: Texting or emailing clients the day before their appointment. Automatable.
- **Invoice and payment follow-up**: Sending a reminder when a payment is overdue. Automatable.
- **New client onboarding**: Sending welcome emails, intake forms, and next steps when someone becomes a customer. Automatable.
- **Review requests**: Asking satisfied customers to leave a Google review after a job is complete. Automatable.
- **Reporting**: Pulling weekly numbers from your tools into a summary. Automatable.

The goal is not to automate everything at once. It is to identify the two or three processes eating the most time and build reliable workflows around them first.

---

## What business process automation is not

It is not buying a SaaS tool and hoping it works. Most automation tools require configuration, integration with your existing systems, and ongoing maintenance. Without someone who knows what they are doing, you end up with a half-built workflow that breaks and nobody fixes.

It is also not a one-time project. Automation needs to be monitored, adjusted as your business changes, and expanded as you find new opportunities.

## What it actually costs for a Denver small business

Business process automation for a small Denver business typically falls into three buckets:

**Software costs**: Most automation runs on tools like Make, Zapier, or n8n. Depending on your volume and complexity, expect $20-100/month in tool costs. This is minimal compared to the time saved.

**Setup costs**: Building the initial workflows, connecting your tools, and testing everything. For a small business, a focused automation build typically runs $1,500-4,000 depending on complexity.

**Ongoing support**: Monthly retainer to monitor, adjust, and expand your automations. Usually $300-800/month for a small business.

The math works quickly. If automation saves a team member 10 hours a week at $20/hour, that is $800/month in recovered capacity - often more than the cost of the retainer.

## Where Denver businesses typically start

The highest-ROI starting points we see across Denver industries:

**Restaurants and food service**: Automated review requests after dining, catering inquiry follow-up, reservation confirmations.

**Home services (HVAC, plumbing, landscaping)**: Estimate follow-up sequences, appointment reminders, review requests after job completion.

**Real estate**: Speed-to-lead automation for new property inquiries, past client check-ins, listing update notifications.

**Medical and dental practices**: Appointment reminders, recall campaigns for patients due for checkups, post-visit follow-up.

**Professional services**: New lead intake and qualification, proposal follow-up, client onboarding sequences.
